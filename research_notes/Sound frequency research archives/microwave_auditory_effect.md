# Microwave Auditory Effect (Frey Effect) and Directed-Energy Auditory Research

Scope note: research completed on 2026-10-25. Several primary-source domains (dni.gov, spectrum.ieee.org, wikipedia.org, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) could not be fetched from the research environment, so some findings come from search-result summaries of those pages or from reputable secondary reporting. These are flagged. Confidence labels: **[High]** means primary or multiple independent reputable sources agree. **[Medium]** means one reputable secondary source, or a primary source seen only in summary. **[Low]** means a single weak or secondary source with no corroboration. Device-relevant engineering parameters are left out on purpose. Mechanisms are described only at a conceptual level.

## 1. Frey's discovery and the thermoelastic mechanism: what is known about thresholds, loudness and power

### Takeaway
Since the 1960s–70s it has been well established that short, high-peak-power RF/microwave pulses can be "heard" as clicks, buzzes or chirps. The accepted mechanism is thermoelastic expansion. Each pulse heats tissue in the head by a tiny, rapid amount (on the order of a millionth of a degree), which launches a pressure wave that the cochlea detects as sound. Perception depends on energy per pulse, not average power. At or near threshold the perceived sound is weak, and making it loud would take energy per pulse high enough that heating becomes a hazard.

### Cited Findings
- **[High]** Allan H. Frey, "Human auditory system response to modulated electromagnetic energy," *Journal of Applied Physiology* 17(4):689–692, 1962 (PMID 13895081). This is the founding paper. The PubMed record has no abstract. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13895081/)
- **[Medium]** Frey reported that subjects heard sounds (described as buzzing, clicking or hissing) during exposure to pulsed RF at low *average* power density. He found that *peak* power density, not average, determined whether the sound was perceived, and he argued that sites peripheral to the cochlea were ruled out. — [Kook Science summary](https://hatch.kookscience.com/wiki/Microwave_auditory_effect_(Frey)); [Elder & Chou 2003 review (mirror)](https://waves.lima-city.de/cms/../dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf)
- **[Low/conflicting]** Secondary copies of Frey's paper disagree on the carrier frequencies he used: one gives two UHF/L-band frequencies, while an OCR'd reproduction shows a different, apparently garbled figure. The original scan should be checked before quoting frequencies. — [Kook Science](https://hatch.kookscience.com/wiki/Microwave_auditory_effect_(Frey)); [Geocities reproduction](https://www.geocities.ws/capaliwoda/mc/Frey_paper.html)
- **[Medium]** Claims, often repeated, that deaf people could hear the effect come from Frey's early reports. Later work tied perception to an intact cochlea: people with conductive (middle-ear) hearing loss could perceive it, but the effect needs working inner-ear and neural pathways. — [Kook Science](https://hatch.kookscience.com/wiki/Microwave_auditory_effect_(Frey)); [Elder & Chou 2003](https://waves.lima-city.de/cms/../dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf)
- **[High]** Mechanism: the accepted explanation is the thermoelastic, rate-of-heating hypothesis advanced by K. R. Foster and E. D. Finch ("Microwave hearing: evidence for thermoacoustic auditory stimulation by pulsed microwaves," *Science* 185:256–258, 1974). Absorbed pulse energy causes rapid, minute thermal expansion, which produces an acoustic (pressure) wave in the head that reaches the cochlea by bone conduction. — [arXiv critique describing the Foster & Finch hypothesis as the standard account](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0102007v11); [ARPANSA review](https://www.arpansa.gov.au/sites/default/files/legacy/pubs/rps/aud_perc.pdf)
- **[High]** James C. Lin (University of Illinois Chicago) carried out the main theoretical and experimental development of the thermoelastic model in the 1970s and onward. This includes his monograph *Microwave Auditory Effects and Applications* (1978) and many reviews in *IEEE Microwave Magazine* ("Health Matters" column) and elsewhere. — [Frontiers in Public Health, Foster et al. 2021 (citing Lin)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.788613/pdf); [Elder & Chou 2003](https://waves.lima-city.de/cms/../dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf)
- **[High]** The temperature rise per pulse at the threshold of perception is extraordinarily small. Reviews give figures around 10⁻⁶ °C (for example, a calculated ~5 × 10⁻⁶ °C), far below anything felt as warmth. — [ARPANSA](https://www.arpansa.gov.au/sites/default/files/legacy/pubs/rps/aud_perc.pdf); [Elder & Chou 2003](https://waves.lima-city.de/cms/../dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf)
- **[High]** Perception depends on energy per pulse (specific absorption per pulse), and mainly on the energy delivered near the leading edge of short pulses, not on time-averaged power density. — [ARPANSA](https://www.arpansa.gov.au/sites/default/files/legacy/pubs/rps/aud_perc.pdf)
- **[High]** Perceived pitch is set largely by the acoustic resonance of the head, so it depends on head size, rather than by the RF carrier frequency. The sound is heard as coming from inside or just behind the head. — [Elder & Chou 2003](https://waves.lima-city.de/cms/../dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf)
- **[Medium]** Loudness and hazard: Foster, Garrett & Ziskin (2021) argue that the effect is weak near threshold and that producing loud or harmful sound would require energy per pulse at levels where tissue heating becomes the dominant hazard. On that basis they consider it implausible as a covert weapon. — [Foster, Garrett & Ziskin, "Can the Microwave Auditory Effect Be 'Weaponized'?", Front. Public Health 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.788613/pdf); [Caltech record](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20220120-890644000)
- **[Medium]** Dissent on mechanism: a long-running arXiv paper (Hyland) argues that the theoretical and experimental support for the Foster–Finch rate-of-heating hypothesis is "weaker than usually assumed." This is a minority view. — [arXiv physics/0102007](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0102007v11)
- **[Medium]** A 2020 arXiv preprint proposes the cochlea itself as the demodulating site. This is an alternative or refinement and has not displaced the thermoelastic model. — [arXiv 2010.02397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.02397)

### Inferences
- The effect is real and reproducible physics. What is disputed is its intensity limits and whether it can be weaponized, not whether it exists.
- Because the effect scales with energy per pulse while hazard scales with deposited energy, clearly audible or speech-like perception and tissue heating risk are physically linked. This is the core of the "it would cook you first" argument.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full text of Frey 1962 or Foster & Finch 1974 to quote their exact numeric thresholds. The figures above come from reviews.
- I found no peer-reviewed, controlled human study demonstrating sustained or intelligible loud speech via this effect.

## 2. Sharp & Grove (Walter Reed, 1973): transmission of spoken words

### Takeaway
Nearly all citations go back to Don R. Justesen's 1975 *American Psychologist* article "Microwaves and Behavior," which describes unpublished experiments by Joseph C. Sharp and Mark Grove at the Walter Reed Army Institute of Research. In those experiments, words were encoded as pulsed microwaves and the subjects reportedly recognized simple spoken digits. I found no primary Sharp & Grove publication. The claim is a documented secondhand report, not a published, replicated experiment.

### Cited Findings
- **[Medium]** Justesen, D. R., "Microwaves and Behavior," *American Psychologist* 30(3):391–401 (March 1975), is the source usually cited. — [Japanese wiki mirror citing Justesen](https://ja.wikid.org/%E3%83%9E%E3%82%A4%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AD%E6%B3%A2%E8%81%B4%E8%A6%9A%E5%8A%B9%E6%9E%9C)
- **[Low–Medium]** As relayed from Justesen: Sharp and Grove (c. 1973, Walter Reed; some accounts say ARPA-related work) converted recordings of spoken digit words (one to ten) into pulse trains. Listeners identified these monosyllabic words, and the sound was likened to speech through an artificial larynx. — [ja.wikid.org](https://ja.wikid.org/%E3%83%9E%E3%82%A4%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AD%E6%B3%A2%E8%81%B4%E8%A6%9A%E5%8A%B9%E6%9E%9C)
- **[Low–Medium]** The same secondary account says longer words or sentences were not tried because the required exposure would approach the safety limits then in force. — [ja.wikid.org](https://ja.wikid.org/%E3%83%9E%E3%82%A4%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AD%E6%B3%A2%E8%81%B4%E8%A6%9A%E5%8A%B9%E6%9E%9C)
- **[Low]** Sources conflict on the date: some online sources attribute the demonstration to Sharp in 1975 rather than 1973. — [HN comment](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/48104041)

### Inferences
- The experiment appears to have been an informal, small-scale laboratory demonstration. Its own reported limitation (exposure limits) is consistent with the physics in Section 1.
- The claim is often stretched into "the military proved voice-to-skull works." The documented record supports only recognition of simple isolated words under laboratory conditions.

### Gaps
- No primary Walter Reed report, data or peer-reviewed paper by Sharp & Grove was found. Justesen's original text was not retrieved directly. Exact wording should be checked in *Am. Psychol.* 1975.

## 3. Documented US government programs and documents

### Takeaway
Two items are well documented. First, a Navy-funded small-business concept called "MEDUSA" (WaveBand Corp. c. 2003–2004, later associated with Sierra Nevada Corp. in 2008 reporting) proposed a non-lethal device based on the microwave auditory effect. It did not advance beyond early-phase funding, and biophysicists judged it unworkable. Second, a 1998 US Army document, "Bioeffects of Selected Non-Lethal Weapons," released via FOIA around 2006–2008, discusses microwave hearing as a phenomenon of possible non-lethal interest, attributes it to thermoelastic expansion, and reports no permanent effects. Neither shows a deployed capability.

### Cited Findings
- **[Medium]** WaveBand Corp. held a US Navy contract in 2003–2004 to design a microwave-auditory-effect system called MEDUSA ("Mob Excess Deterrent Using Silent Audio"). Sierra Nevada Corp. reportedly took over the work. — [search summary of Wikipedia "Microwave auditory effect"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_auditory_effect); [New Atlas 2008](https://newatlas.com/medusa-microwave-crowd-control-raygun/9605/)
- **[Medium]** In July 2008 *New Scientist* reported that Sierra Nevada Corp. planned to build MEDUSA under a Navy contract following tests. ABC News relayed the report. — [ABC News 2008](https://abcnews.go.com/Technology/AheadoftheCurve/story?id=5305386)
- **[Medium]** IEEE Spectrum (2008) reported that biophysics experts said the device "cannot work" because it would cause dangerous heating before the noise became bothersome, and that MEDUSA did not receive Navy funding beyond the first (SBIR-style) phase. Kenneth Foster was among the skeptics. — [IEEE Spectrum, "Why the Microwave Auditory Effect Crowd-Control Gun Won't Work" (seen via search summary)](https://spectrum.ieee.org/why-microwave-auditory-effect-crowdcontrol-gun-wont-work)
- **[Medium]** A US Army report, "Bioeffects of Selected Non-Lethal Weapons" (1998), was released to a member of the public under FOIA and publicized in February 2008. It describes RF hearing, says animal studies (guinea pigs, cats) point to thermoelastic expansion, says exposures cause no permanent effects, and mentions potential communication with individuals at a distance (reportedly up to several hundred meters). — [Phys.org 2008](https://phys.org/news/2008-02-pentagon-lasers-voices.html); [RCFP "FOIA of the Weird"](https://www.rcfp.org/foia-weird-ray-guns-and-vaporized-shoes/)
- **[Low]** One source misdates the document as 1988, and a wiki claims a declassification date of December 2006. Neither is verified. — [RCFP](https://www.rcfp.org/foia-weird-ray-guns-and-vaporized-shoes/); [ja.wikid.org](https://ja.wikid.org/%E3%83%9E%E3%82%A4%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AD%E6%B3%A2%E8%81%B4%E8%A6%9A%E5%8A%B9%E6%9E%9C)
- **[Medium]** The Joint Non-Lethal Weapons program has published material on high-energy and RF non-lethal concepts, for example the HEAP document hosted at jifco.defense.gov. This shows ongoing DoD interest in RF bioeffects for non-lethal purposes, and the best-known fielded example is the millimeter-wave Active Denial System (a skin-heating system, not auditory). — [JIFCO HEAP PDF](https://jifco.defense.gov/Portals/50/Documents/Future_Non-Lethal_Weapons/HEAP.pdf); [FAS](https://fas.org/publication/radio_frequency_bioeffects_vie/)
- **[Medium]** Historical context: the early research was largely military-sponsored. Frey worked at General Electric's Advanced Electronics Center, and later RF-hearing research was funded by the US Navy (ONR), the US Air Force and the US Army (WRAIR). — [Elder & Chou 2003](https://waves.lima-city.de/cms/../dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf); [FAS](https://fas.org/publication/radio_frequency_bioeffects_vie/)

### Inferences
- The documentary record shows interest and early-stage concepts, not a demonstrated or fielded auditory weapon. The MEDUSA funding outcome and the expert critique fit the physics constraints in Section 1.
- The 1998 Army document is a literature-based assessment. It is evidence that the effect was considered, not evidence of a working system.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the full 1998 Army document or MEDUSA SBIR contract records. The original 2008 *New Scientist* article was not fetched.
- I found no reliable public record of any further US program after 2008 specifically on auditory-effect devices.

## 4. Relevance to Havana Syndrome / Anomalous Health Incidents (AHIs)

### Takeaway
Official assessments have split. The 2020 National Academies committee and a 2022 IC experts panel judged directed pulsed RF energy a plausible or "most plausible" explanation for a *subset* of cases. In contrast, 2023 and 2025 IC assessments judged a foreign-adversary weapon "very unlikely" (with two agencies dissenting in 2025), and 2024 NIH studies found no MRI-detectable brain injury. In June 2026 DNI Gabbard rescinded the skeptical assessments on process grounds without issuing a new causal finding. James Lin has argued that the pulsed-microwave hypothesis is plausible. Kenneth Foster and colleagues argue it is physically implausible. Causation remains unresolved and contested.

### Cited Findings
- **[High]** NASEM (December 2020), *An Assessment of Illness in U.S. Government Employees and Their Families at Overseas Embassies*, prepared for the State Department by a 19-member committee. It found that "directed, pulsed radiofrequency energy appears to be the most plausible mechanism" for the distinctive early symptoms (sudden sound or pressure sensation, head pressure, dizziness, cognitive problems). It said psychological and other factors may have contributed, did not name a source or conclude there was an attack, and noted that some classified information was not available to it. The State Department called "consistent with" a term of art that does not assign cause. — [KPBS/NPR](https://www.kpbs.org/news/2020/12/06/microwave-radiation-most-plausible-cause-of); [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2020/12/05/world/report-points-microwave-attack-likely-source-mystery-illnesses-that-hit-diplomats-spies); report at nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/25889
- **[High]** IC Experts Panel (February 2022; a 153-page version was later declassified after a James Madison Project lawsuit). It concluded that pulsed electromagnetic energy, particularly pulsed RF, "plausibly explains the core characteristics" of a small subset of cases, and that ultrasound could do so in limited close-range cases. It did not determine whether attacks happened or who was responsible. Psychosocial factors alone could not explain the core cases. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/national-security/havana-syndrome-symptoms-small-group-likely-caused-directed-energy-say-rcna14584); [Yahoo/declassified report coverage](https://news.yahoo.com/newly-declassified-report-contradicts-officials-043000559.html)
- **[Medium]** A declassified 2018 State Department JASON-related analysis concluded that the recorded Havana sounds were most likely crickets (the Indies short-tailed cricket). — [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/politics/politics-news/havana-syndrome-crickets-not-microwaves-report-1235163/); [National Interest](https://nationalinterest.org/node/194849)
- **[High]** March 2023 IC assessment: five agencies judged it "very unlikely" that a foreign adversary was responsible. (This finding was carried into the 2024 update.) — [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/us-intelligence-mostly-rejects-links-between-havana-syndrome-adversaries/7932763.html)
- **[High]** January 2025 (NIC updated ICA, dated December 2024): five agencies still judged foreign-adversary responsibility "very unlikely." Two agencies shifted to a "roughly even chance" that a foreign adversary had developed or used a novel weapon (with one of them focused on the possibility of development rather than use). Officials said analysts could not rule out a small number of cases. — [NIC Unclassified ICA Dec 2024 (PDF; not fetchable here)](https://dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-ICA-Updated-Assessment-AHI-December2024.pdf); [VOA via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/intell/library/news/2025/intell-250110-voa01.htm); [Anadolu](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/americas/most-us-intelligence-agencies-see-no-link-between-foreign-adversaries-and-havana-syndrome/3447228); [Sen. Warner statement](https://www.warner.senate.gov/public/index.cfm/2025/1/sen-warner-on-anomalous-health-incidents-ahi-update)
- **[High]** A December 2024 House Intelligence subcommittee report argued that the IC had pursued a "politically palatable" conclusion. — [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/us-intelligence-mostly-rejects-links-between-havana-syndrome-adversaries/7932763.html)
- **[High]** June 12, 2026: DNI Tulsi Gabbard rescinded the two Biden-era skeptical assessments. An ODNI memo said they had selectively excluded relevant intelligence, suppressed alternative analysis, relied on an "ethically flawed" medical study, and limited collection "to maintain an analytic line which relied on absence of evidence." No new causal conclusion was issued, and it was unclear whether a new investigation would follow. Gabbard was departing, and Jay Clayton was nominated as successor. — [CNN via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/politics/national-politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/06/12/gabbard-rescinds-biden-era-intel-assessments-that-were-skeptical-about-havana-syndrome/); [AP via Hawaii Tribune-Herald](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/2026/06/12/nation-world-news/gabbard-revokes-biden-era-assessments-on-mysterious-ailments/); [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/world/article/gabbard-rescinds-biden-era-intel-assessments-that-were-skeptical-about-havana-syndrome/)
- **[High]** NIH studies (JAMA, March 2024). Pierpaoli et al. (81 AHI participants vs. 48 controls) found no significant MRI differences in volume, diffusion or functional connectivity. Chan et al. found no differences in most biomarkers but worse balance and more fatigue, PTSD and depression symptoms in the AHI group. NIH said the symptoms are "real, distressing" and acknowledged that markers of an external phenomenon might no longer be detectable. — [NIH press release](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-studies-find-severe-symptoms-havana-syndrome-no-evidence-mri-detectable-brain-injury-or-biological-abnormalities); [Applied Radiology](https://appliedradiology.com/Articles/no-mri-detectable-brain-injury-found-in-people-with-havana-syndrome)
- **[Medium]** James C. Lin, "Sonic health attacks by pulsed microwaves in Havana revisited," *IEEE Microwave Magazine* 22:71–73 (2021). Lin argues that pulsed high-power microwaves could plausibly have been delivered covertly and that the induced sound pressure could approach or exceed discomfort levels and possibly cause tissue injury. He had written earlier pieces on Havana from 2017–2018 onward. — [Cited in Frontiers/PMC summaries](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.788613/pdf)
- **[Medium]** Foster, Garrett & Ziskin (2021, *Front. Public Health*) argue that weaponizing the effect is implausible: the intense exposures needed would produce heating and detectable effects, and no microwave exposure was detected. They do not speculate on what did cause the symptoms. A 2023 follow-up commentary continues the debate. Foster was quoted in 2023: "It isn't even close to a plausible theory." — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.788613/pdf); [Frontiers 2023 commentary](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2022.1118762/pdf); [Caltech record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/pyc82-w6d14)

### Inferences
- The "most plausible" language from 2020 and 2022 is comparative and hedged. It means plausible relative to other candidate explanations for a subset of cases, not proof of an attack or a device.
- The 2026 rescission was procedural and political. It reopens the question but adds no new physical evidence that pulsed RF was used.
- The microwave auditory effect explains, in principle, a perceived sound. It does not by itself explain lasting injury, which is where Foster's and Lin's positions diverge most.

### Gaps
- I could not read the NASEM report, the ODNI PDF or the IC panel report in full from this environment.
- I found no public evidence (instrument recordings, recovered hardware) confirming RF exposure in any AHI case.
- Whether a new post-rescission IC review was started after June 2026 is unknown as of this research.

## 5. Safety standards (IEEE C95.1 / ICNIRP) and pulsed-RF auditory effects; electromagnetic vs. acoustic

### Takeaway
The current RF safety standards (IEEE C95.1-2019 and ICNIRP 2020) cover 0 Hz–300 GHz and explicitly consider microwave hearing when setting limits for pulsed fields. The approach includes per-pulse or short-duration energy limits alongside time-averaged SAR limits. Standards bodies generally treat RF hearing as a perceptible biological effect rather than an adverse health effect. Lin has criticized the standards as inadequate for pulsed exposures, and standards-committee members dispute his critique. The effect is purely electromagnetic: the sound is generated inside the head's tissue, so loudspeakers, ultrasound emitters or other acoustic devices cannot produce the Frey effect.

### Cited Findings
- **[High]** IEEE C95.1-2019 covers 0 Hz–300 GHz, targets established adverse effects, and sets limits for restricted (occupational) and unrestricted (public) environments. — [IEEE Access synopsis (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/c65c4da3987e493684b655bae4747d7c)
- **[Medium]** Slides from the IEEE C95.1 chair (C.-K. Chou, 2019) list microwave hearing among the effects the standard considered. — [IEEE OEB slides](https://ewh.ieee.org/r6/oeb/lm/previous_meetings/2019/Chou%20May%2029%202019.pdf)
- **[Medium]** ARPANSA's review explains the role of peak power and pulse energy in RF hearing, which is the rationale for pulse-based limits. This is an older document that predates the 2019 and 2020 standards. — [ARPANSA](https://www.arpansa.gov.au/sites/default/files/legacy/pubs/rps/aud_perc.pdf)
- **[Medium]** 2026 dispute: Bailey, Foster & Chou (Frontiers in Public Health, 2026) respond to Lin's critique of IEEE C95.1-2019 and ICNIRP 2020. They say Lin misstates both limit sets and conflates biological effects with health hazards. The authors are committee members, so this is an interested party's position. — [PMC12961997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12961997/)
- **[High]** Electromagnetic vs. acoustic: the sound arises from RF energy absorbed in tissue and converted to a pressure wave inside the head (thermoelastic), so no airborne sound exists for bystanders or microphones to detect. — [ARPANSA](https://www.arpansa.gov.au/sites/default/files/legacy/pubs/rps/aud_perc.pdf); [Elder & Chou 2003](https://waves.lima-city.de/cms/../dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf)
- **[High]** Corollary: Havana audio recordings captured airborne sound, which was later attributed to crickets. That is by definition not the microwave auditory effect, which produces nothing a microphone can record. — [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/politics/politics-news/havana-syndrome-crickets-not-microwaves-report-1235163/)

### Inferences
- Claims of "voice to skull" via speakers, phone apps or ultrasonic emitters confuse different physical phenomena. Directional audio such as parametric ultrasonic speakers is a separate acoustic technology that produces real airborne sound.
- Under normal RF exposures that comply with the standards (phones, Wi-Fi, broadcast), the per-pulse energy is far below the RF-hearing threshold. RF hearing has been reported mainly near high-peak-power pulsed sources such as radar.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the exact numeric per-pulse limits in IEEE C95.1-2019 or ICNIRP 2020. They are deliberately left out here anyway. The primary texts are IEEE Xplore doc 8859679 and *Health Physics* 118(5), 2020.
- I found no peer-reviewed evidence supporting "voice to skull" harassment claims by individuals. Such claims rest on extrapolation from the documented effect and secondary sources, not on demonstrated capability.
