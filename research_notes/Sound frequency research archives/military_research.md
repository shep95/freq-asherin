## 1. Non-lethal acoustic weapons research (JNLWD, AFRL/Army, NATO, Altmann): conclusions on infrasound, audible high-intensity sound, ultrasound

### Takeaway
The independent scientific assessments agree: infrasound does **not** have the dramatic effects popular accounts claim. The documented harms of acoustic weapons come mainly from very loud **audible** sound (hearing loss, pain, tinnitus), plus nonauditory effects only at extreme levels (above about 150 dB in the low audio range). Reaching disabling levels beyond about 50 m from a meter-sized source looks impractical. NATO's human-effects group found the quality data base "very little" and much of it restricted. US DoD (JNLWD) still describes acoustic hailing devices mainly as hail-and-warn tools and continues to fund human-effects research.

### Cited Findings
- **[HIGH] Altmann, J. (2001), "Acoustic Weapons – A Prospective Assessment," *Science & Global Security* 9:165–234.** Conclusions: infrasound, prominent in journalistic articles, "does not have the alleged drastic effects on humans". Sound levels producing aural pain, equilibrium problems or other profound effects "seem unachievable at ranges above about 50 m for meter-size sources". Hearing damage is the main risk: temporary threshold shifts can become permanent depending on level, frequency and duration, and at very high levels even one or a few short exposures can cause partial or full deafness — [Science & Global Security archive](https://scienceandglobalsecurity.org/archive/2001/11/acoustic_weapons_-_a_prospecti.html) (abstract via search; full text not fetched). The volume and page numbers come from my own knowledge and were not verified on the fetched page.
- **[MEDIUM] Altmann's 1999 review (presented at the Acoustical Society of America, 137th meeting):** infrasound allegations "cannot be confirmed"; marked nonauditory effects exist in the low audio range at levels above 150 dB — [ASA Pressroom, 137th meeting](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/137th/altmann.html); [Sonicfield library copy](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/acoustic-weapons-effects-of-very-high-sound-levels)
- **[MEDIUM]** Altmann, asked about the 2017 Havana (Cuba) incidents, said he knew of no acoustic effect that would produce concussion-like symptoms — [Fox News](https://foxnews.com/tech/sonic-weapons-reported-in-cuba-could-sound-be-harnessed-in-an-attack.amp)
- **[HIGH] NATO RTO TR-HFM-073, "Human Effects of Non-Lethal Technologies" (final report, August 2006).** The executive summary found no suitable scheme for organizing data on the human effects of non-lethal technologies, that much existing data was restricted for proprietary or national-security reasons, and that "there is yet very little relevant quality data available". It recommended a multi-tiered database — [NATO STO TR-HFM-073 PDF](https://www.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Technical%20Reports/RTO-TR-HFM-073/$$TR-HFM-073-ALL.pdf). The report's acoustic-specific conclusions were not retrieved (see Gaps). Related NATO lecture series: RTO-EN-HFM-145 — [EN-HFM-145 ES](https://www.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Educational%20Notes/RTO-EN-HFM-145/$EN-HFM-145-ES.pdf); [EN-HFM-145-04](https://www.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Educational%20Notes/RTO-EN-HFM-145/EN-HFM-145-04.pdf). Also NATO RTO-TR-SAS-040 (non-lethal weapons) — [PDF](https://www.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Technical%20Reports/RTO-TR-SAS-040/TR-SAS-040-$$ALL.pdf)
- **[MEDIUM] US National Academies (NRC, *An Assessment of Non-Lethal Weapons Science and Technology*, 2003):** acoustic-weapon effectiveness remains unproven despite considerable study; results questionable and effects unclear — [NAP chapter](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10538/chapter/9)
- **[HIGH] JNLWD/JIFCO (US DoD) fact sheet, "Distributed Sound and Light Array":** the system is "undergoing human effects and effectiveness testing". The program draws on earlier research into sound and light levels that harm the eye and ear. "The Joint Non-Lethal Weapons Program continues to fund human effects and effectiveness research to better quantify the military usefulness of optical distracters and acoustic hailing devices" — [JIFCO fact sheet](https://jifco.defense.gov/Press-Room/Fact-Sheets/Article-View-Fact-sheets/Article/577990/distributed-sound-and-light-array); program page — [JIFCO Non-Lethal Capabilities](https://jifco.defense.gov/Non-Lethal-Capabilities-and-Requirements/)
- **[MEDIUM]** In a 2009 USMC/JNLWD demonstration at Quantico, an acoustic hailing device relayed intelligible messages at 100, 200 and 300 m — [GlobalSecurity (USMC news reprint)](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2009/04/mil-090402-mcn01.htm)
- **[MEDIUM]** A Navy SBIR topic, N202-089 "Focused Enhanced Acoustic-Driver Technologies (FEAT) for Long Range Non-Lethal Hail and Warn," shows continued DoD investment in longer-range hail/warn acoustics (2020) — [NavySBIR N202-089](https://www.navysbir.com/n20_2/N202-089.htm)
- **[MEDIUM]** National Defense magazine (Feb 2020) argued that more research into the human impacts of high-intensity audible sound was needed — [National Defense, Feb 2020 p.15](https://digital.nationaldefensemagazine.org/february-2020/page-15)
- **[MEDIUM]** A DTIC report on non-lethal acoustics was located but not read: [ADA436202](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA436202.pdf)
- **[MEDIUM]** Bradford University (UK) Non-Lethal Weapons research project voiced concern that acoustic weapons such as LRAD were being "rushed" into service without thorough health testing (authors' view) — [Bradford Scholars](https://bradscholars.brad.ac.uk/entities/publication/cf9facca-c135-4254-a614-0725f3aad515)
- **[MEDIUM]** Legal analysis (Bill Boothby, 2025): pain-inflicting sonic devices raise legality questions under international humanitarian law — [Duke LawFire](https://sites.duke.edu/lawfire/2025/03/20/guest-post-bill-boothby-on-are-sonic-weapons-legal/)

### Inferences
- The independent literature (Altmann, NRC 2003, NATO HFM-073) points one way: claims of incapacitating infrasound are unsupported, and the real-world hazard is hearing damage from high-level audible sound at close range.
- Much DoD human-effects data appears to be unpublished or restricted (NATO HFM-073 says so explicitly). Public conclusions therefore rest heavily on independent academics.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full text of Altmann 2001 or the acoustic chapter of NATO TR-HFM-073 (host DNS failures). I could not quote their exact dB thresholds for ultrasound, blast or the pain threshold.
- Found no publicly accessible Air Force Research Laboratory or Army Research Laboratory acoustic-bioeffects report in this session. The Penn State Applied Research Laboratory link to JNLWD acoustic human-effects work was not confirmed.
- Ultrasound: no source retrieved here gives military findings. Altmann is generally understood to conclude that airborne ultrasound couples poorly into the body and attenuates quickly in air. That comes from my own knowledge and was not verified in this session.

## 2. Vladimir Gavreau's 1960s infrasound research (France): documented vs. legend

### Takeaway
Gavreau (CNRS lab, Marseille) did build high-intensity low-frequency and acoustic sources in the 1960s, and published on them (Gavreau et al. 1966; a 1968 *Science Journal* article). There is little evidence beyond that. He made no controlled trials or proper measurements, and he confused the effects of high-level audible sound (one whistle was about 2,600 Hz, which is not infrasound) with infrasound. The lethal "7 Hz" claims and the technician who "fell down dead" are press and 1970s-book embellishments that have never been substantiated.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** Origin stories conflict. One version is ear pain and shaking equipment with no audible sound on microphones. Another is nausea traced to a "loosely poised low speed motor" or ventilator fan (1957 in some accounts). The fan story comes from popular media of the time — [Wikipedia: Vladimir Gavreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Gavreau); [Psi Encyclopedia, Infrasound (PDF)](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/sites/default/files/ebook/article/infrasound-191.pdf)
- **[HIGH] Leventhall (acoustician) review, "Thirty-five years of low frequency noise" (Institute of Acoustics proceedings):** Gavreau "developed high intensity sources, but made some unwise statements which confused harmful effects of very high levels at higher frequencies with the effects of infrasound". The press picked these up and embellished them further, including a claim that 7 Hz was fatal — [IOA proceedings PDF](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/g_leventhall_thirty_five_years_of_low_frequency_noise.pdf)
- **[MEDIUM]** Critics note he tested only short bursts on himself and labmates, with no scientific measurements or controlled trials. One whistle was about 2,600 Hz, yet his 1968 paper implied infrasound caused the injuries — [Smithsonian, "You Can't Blow Somebody's Brain Up With Sound"](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/you-cant-blow-somebodys-brain-up-with-sound-141239932); [Acoustics Today article](https://acousticstoday.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Article_3of4_from_ATCODK_9_3.pdf)
- **[MEDIUM]** The claim that a technician "fell down dead on the spot", and the claims of shattered windows and collapsing buildings, trace to a 1973 popular book and have never been substantiated. Testing reportedly stopped because the sounds shook nearby houses and set dogs barking, not because of bodily harm — [Psi Encyclopedia (PDF)](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/sites/default/files/ebook/article/infrasound-191.pdf); [Wikipedia: Infrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrasound)
- **[MEDIUM]** Later research links high-dose infrasound to headaches, nausea and breathing difficulty, but nothing has replicated Gavreau's dramatic "findings" — [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/you-cant-blow-somebodys-brain-up-with-sound-141239932)

### Inferences
- Gavreau's work is the root of the "infrasound weapon" myth. The documented core (high-intensity whistles, some discomfort) is modest, and the legend (lethal 7 Hz, deaths, structural collapse) is folklore.

### Gaps
- Primary Gavreau papers (1966 *Acustica*; 1968 *Science Journal*) were not retrieved. Whether the work had French military funding is not documented in the sources found.
- The name of the 1973 book is not given in the snippets (likely Lyall Watson's *Supernature*, but unverified here).

## 3. Long Range Acoustic Devices (LRAD / acoustic hailing devices): origins, frequency band, deployments, injuries, litigation, safety

### Takeaway
Acoustic hailing devices (AHDs) came out of a US Navy requirement after the October 2000 USS Cole bombing in Aden, Yemen: crews could not be sure an approaching boat could hear their warnings. LRAD Corporation (now Genasys) filled the requirement. The devices are highly directional and work in the main band of human hearing (manufacturer: 1–5 kHz; about 2 kHz typical). Police use has caused documented hearing injuries. That led to the Pittsburgh G-20 settlement (2012) and to *Edrei v. Maguire / Bratton* (2d Cir. 2018), which held that using an LRAD on non-violent protesters in a way capable of causing serious injury can be unconstitutional excessive force.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** The term "acoustic hailing device" came into common use after the USS Cole attack in Yemen in 2000, after which the US Navy set a requirement for such a device. The crew had been unable to be sure their messages could be heard by the approaching boat at a distance — [Wikipedia: Long-range acoustic device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-range_acoustic_device). Casualty figures differ: 17 killed, with 37, 39 or 40 injured depending on source — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-range_acoustic_device); [Romanian Naval Academy bulletin 2018](https://www.anmb.ro/buletinstiintific/buletine/2018_Issue1/03_NTM/134.pdf)
- **[LOW, vendor]** Genasys (formerly LRAD Corp., San Diego) says it created the AHD market. It says its systems are optimized for the 1–5 kHz band of human hearing and broadcast in a 30° beam — [Genasys blog](https://genasys.com/blog/lrad-is-the-de-facto-standard-for-long-range-hailing-devices-and-heres-why); [Genasys Naval Port Applications PDF](https://genasys.com/content/uploads/2024/05/LRAD-Naval-Port-Applications-Systems_.pdf)
- **[MEDIUM]** Directional AHD beams are typically 5°–60° conical at a 2 kHz tone — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-range_acoustic_device). The often-quoted "2.5 kHz" figure was not confirmed in the sources retrieved.
- **[MEDIUM]** Maritime anti-piracy use: LRAD was used to help ward off attackers on cruise ships (e.g., the 2005 Seabourn Spirit attack off Somalia, per San Diego Business Journal) — [SDBJ](https://www.sdbj.com/?p=11444). The incident name comes from my own knowledge; the article title is "Technology Made Here Helps Ward Off Cruise Ship Attackers."
- **[HIGH] Pittsburgh G-20, 24 Sept 2009:** police used an LRAD. Bystander Karen Piper, a visiting professor at Carnegie Mellon, reported permanent hearing damage. In November 2012 the city settled her claim for $72,000 without admitting wrongdoing. It also agreed to meet a consultant on LRAD use (with no obligation to follow the advice). The suit formally ended August 2013. The two-case settlement totalled about $200,000–$215,000 — [ACLU-PA press release](https://www.aclupa.org/press-releases/city-pittsburgh-settles-g-20-lawsuits/); [WESA 2012](https://www.wesa.fm/politics-government/2012-11-14/city-settles-g20-suits-for-215-000); [WESA 2013](https://wesa.fm/identity-justice/2013-08-20/pittsburgh-g20-hearing-loss-suit-officially-over); [TribLive](https://archive.triblive.com/news/pittsburgh-to-pay-researcher-who-suffered-hearing-loss-during-g-20-summit). The 2009 G-20 is widely reported as the first US use of LRAD on protesters; that point is not verified in these sources.
- **[HIGH] *Edrei v. Maguire* (also captioned *Edrei v. Bratton*), 2d Cir. No. 17-2065, decided 13 June 2018 (not 2019).** Facts: during a December 2014 protest in Manhattan after the Eric Garner grand jury decision, the NYPD used an LRAD on protesters and journalists. Injuries included "auditory pain, migraines, tinnitus, and hearing loss". Holding: "purposefully using a LRAD in a manner capable of causing serious injury to move non-violent protesters to the sidewalks" violates the Fourteenth Amendment under clearly established law. Qualified immunity was denied to Lt. John Maguire and Officer Mike Poletto. Judge Katzmann stressed the court was not barring LRAD use outright. Rehearing en banc was denied 30 Aug 2018. The cert stage was SCOTUS No. 18-810 (*Maguire v. Edrei*) — [Opinion PDF via SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/18-810-opinion-below.pdf); [SCOTUS docket filing](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/18/18-810/72281/20181115165108699_Maguire%20v%20Edrei%20-%20Application%20for%20Extension%20of%20Time%20to%20Seek%20Certiorari.pdf); [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/second-circuit-gets-loud-with-nypd-on-sound-cannons/); [Criminal Legal News](https://www.criminallegalnews.org/news/2018/nov/6/second-circuit-denies-nypd-qualified-immunity-use-military-grade-acoustic-weapon-peaceful-protesters/); [Police1](https://police1.com/police-products/less-lethal/articles/federal-appeals-panel-lrads-can-be-considered-excessive-force-eIivtcdvFrp3gm2i)
- **[HIGH] PHR and INCLO, *Lethal in Disguise: The Health Consequences of Crowd-Control Weapons* (2016; 2nd edition 2023).** Acoustic weapons can cause pain and hearing damage, and the narrow beam does not prevent harm to bystanders and police. Ear protection may not be enough. Research on health effects "is still lacking". The report recommends suspending acoustic weapons at protests until concerns are addressed. It includes a New York case study — [Lethal in Disguise: acoustic weapons](https://lethalindisguise.org/crowd-control-weapons/acoustic-weapons/); [NY case study](https://lethalindisguise.org/case-studies/united-states-new-york/); [PHR acoustic weapons resource](https://phr.org/our-work/resources/health-impacts-of-crowd-control-weapons-acoustic-weapons/); [ACLU Acoustic Weapons PDF](https://assets.aclu.org/live/uploads/document/Acoustic_Weapons.pdf)
- **[MEDIUM]** Reported effect thresholds in legal and medical commentary: discomfort, permanent hearing loss at or above 130 dB, and loss of balance at higher levels, depending on settings, direction and distance — [MDLinx](https://mdlinx.com/article/sonic-weapons-and-their-shocking-health-toll-what-doctors-should-know/1ruKC67O0ThztR0qrFF2Ww); [Duke LawFire](https://sites.duke.edu/lawfire/2025/03/20/guest-post-bill-boothby-on-are-sonic-weapons-legal/)
- **[MEDIUM]** A 2024 peer-reviewed article on acoustic hailing devices was located but not read: [Informa/Taylor & Francis 10.1080/20551940.2024.2304423](https://www.informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/20551940.2024.2304423)
- **[MEDIUM]** Whether any federal circuit other than the Second Circuit has ruled on LRAD: an aggregator fact-check was found but not verified — [factually.co](https://factually.co/fact-checks/justice/federal-circuits-rulings-lrad-acoustic-crowd-control-devices-ed7ddb)

### Inferences
- The LRAD's military purpose (hail and warn at sea) and its police use (crowd dispersal using the "deterrent tone") are different uses. The documented injuries come from the second, at close range.
- The *Edrei* ruling is the leading US precedent. It treats LRAD as a use of force subject to excessive-force analysis without banning the device.

### Gaps
- No DoD or JNLWD formal hearing-safety evaluation of LRAD output (a published DTIC report) was retrieved. The NYPD's 2014 internal LRAD test report is often cited but was not found here.
- Other documented deployments (Iraq or Afghanistan checkpoints, the 2016 Standing Rock protests, 2020 US protests, 2023 Serbia and 2024–25 Georgia allegations) were not researched. They are reported in the press, but not verified in this session.
- The outcome of the *Maguire v. Edrei* cert petition (No. 18-810) was not confirmed. I believe cert was denied in 2019, but that is unverified.

## 4. Israel's "The Scream," the "Mosquito" deterrent, and other documented acoustic devices

### Takeaway
The IDF first publicly used a vehicle-mounted crowd-dispersal sound device ("the Scream" / "Screamer") on 3 June 2005 at a protest against the West Bank barrier in Bil'in. Protesters reported dizziness and nausea. Israel never released technical specifications. The UK-made "Mosquito," a high-frequency tone audible mainly to young people, led the Council of Europe Parliamentary Assembly to recommend a Europe-wide ban in public places (Recommendation 1930, 25 June 2010). The ban is advisory and not binding.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** On 3 June 2005 the IDF confirmed first use of a new crowd-dispersal device emitting painful bursts of sound against about 400 protesters at Bil'in — [Palestine Chronology (IPS)](https://chronology.palestine-studies.org/node/4767). AP (10 June 2005): protesters "covered their ears and grabbed their heads, overcome by dizziness and nausea". The vehicle-mounted device sent bursts of audible "but not loud" sound about every 10 seconds. A military official said it targets the inner ear. An IDF spokeswoman said it was used after stone-throwing and might be used on settlers resisting the Gaza disengagement — [GlobalSecurity (AP reprint)](https://www.globalsecurity.org/org/news/2005/050610-sound-weapon.htm); [Military.com DefenseTech](https://www.military.com/defensetech/2005/06/10/israelis-sonic-blaster?amp); [NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/4701588); [StrategyPage](https://www.strategypage.com/htmw/htweap/articles/20050614.aspx)
- **[LOW]** Haaretz, cited by an activist blog, reported about 15 demonstrators and 3 security personnel lightly injured at the 20 June 2005 Bil'in protest where the device was used — [CounterVortex](https://countervortex.org/node/653). Experts said the effects of prolonged exposure were unknown — [GlobalSecurity/AP](https://www.globalsecurity.org/org/news/2005/050610-sound-weapon.htm)
- **[HIGH] Council of Europe PACE, Recommendation 1930 (2010), "Prohibiting the marketing and use of the 'Mosquito' youth dispersal device"** (rapporteur Mr Wach). The Culture, Science and Education Committee voted unanimously in March 2010, and the plenary adopted the text on 25 June 2010. The text calls the device discriminatory toward young people (treating them like "unwanted birds or pests"). It says the device may breach rights to private life and freedom of assembly, and may amount to degrading treatment by inflicting "acoustic pain". It also notes that research so far shows **no danger of hearing loss** from the Mosquito sound, but urges more medical testing under the precautionary principle. The Committee of Ministers replied on 16 Mar 2011 — [PACE news 3000](https://pace.coe.int/news/3000/pace-calls-for-ban-on-acoustic-youth-dispersal-devices); [PACE committee news 2791](https://pace.coe.int/news/2791/pace-committee-calls-for-a-ban-on-mosquito-youth-dispersal-devices-in-all-public-places); [PACE text](https://pace.coe.int/files/17891/html); [Statewatch](https://statewatch.org/news/2010/july/council-of-europe-parliamentary-assembly-prohibiting-the-marketing-and-use-of-the-mosquito-youth-dispersal-device/); [CYP Now](https://cypnow.co.uk/news/article/council-of-europe-assembly-votes-to-ban-use-of-mosquito-dispersal-devices); [EPTA database](https://eptanetwork.org/database/projects/133-mosquito-youth-dispersal-device)

### Inferences
- The Scream and the Mosquito show two separate concerns: aversive *audible* tones used on crowds (pain, nausea, unknown long-term effects), and *age-selective* high-frequency deterrence (a rights and dignity question rather than a demonstrated hearing hazard).

### Gaps
- No primary IDF technical or safety documentation on the Scream exists publicly. Israel declined to release specifications.
- National court rulings on the Mosquito (e.g., in Belgium, France or Germany) and the UK Children's Commissioner's "Buzz Off" campaign (2008) were not retrieved.
- Other devices not researched this session: the JNLWD "Distributed Sound and Light Array" (fact sheet cited above), the 2023 and 2025 allegations of sound-weapon use in Belgrade and Tbilisi, and the "Havana syndrome" acoustic hypothesis. US intelligence assessments in 2023 and 2025 judged a foreign weapon "very unlikely" for most cases; that is not verified here.

## 5. Psychological operations using sound: Wandering Soul, Noriega 1989, Waco 1993, Fallujah, Guantánamo

### Takeaway
Psychological use of sound is well documented. In Vietnam (about 1969–70) the 6th PSYOP Battalion broadcast "Ghost Tape No. 10," exploiting Vietnamese beliefs about wandering souls; its effect is disputed. In Panama (Dec 1989 – Jan 1990), US forces blasted rock music at the Vatican nunciature where Noriega sheltered. At Waco (1993) the FBI broadcast noise over the objections of its own negotiators and behavioral experts. At Guantánamo, official DoD, Army and Senate investigations confirm loud music used as an interrogation technique. Fallujah (2004) music broadcasts are documented mainly through secondary accounts.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEDIUM] Operation Wandering Soul / "Ghost Tape No. 10":** run by the 6th PSYOP Battalion (activated Nov 1965) with Navy help. It played on the belief that dying far from home makes one a wandering spirit. Voice actors played dead soldiers and their families, and the tapes were broadcast at night from helicopters, aircraft and ground speakers. A broadcast around 1970 near Fire Support Base Chamberlain is often cited. Effectiveness is disputed. A former commander said the tapes were not to be played within earshot of friendly South Vietnamese forces. Details such as an added tiger roar are anecdotal — [The War Zone](https://twz.com/42927/ghosts-stories-were-used-by-the-u-s-in-vietnam-to-spook-the-enemy); [Foreign Policy 2023](https://foreignpolicy.com/2023/09/16/vietnam-war-psyops-ghosts/); [Military History Now](https://militaryhistorynow.com/2013/10/30/trick-or-treat-the-strange-tale-of-ghost-tape-no-10/); [HowStuffWorks](https://science.howstuffworks.com/ghost-tape-no-10-haunted-mixtape-the-vietnam-war.htm); [KCRW Here Be Monsters](https://www.kcrw.com/shows/here-be-monsters/stories/hbm055-ghost-tape-number-ten)
- **[HIGH/MEDIUM] Noriega, Panama (Operation Just Cause):** around-the-clock loud rock and metal ("Welcome to the Jungle," "Wanted Dead or Alive," "The End") was played at the Vatican nunciature. Gen. Maxwell Thurman ordered speakers placed as a "musical barrier". Army PSYOP personnel were involved, and troops' requests shaped the playlist. The Vatican and the White House objected, and the music stopped after several days. Noriega surrendered on 3 Jan 1990. The playlist comes from declassified SOUTHCOM documents obtained by the National Security Archive — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/judgment-day-heavy-metal-s-role-manuel-noriega-s-surrender-n766411); [WGBH/PRI](https://www.wgbh.org/news/2017-05-30/how-the-u-s-military-used-guns-n-roses-to-make-a-dictator-give-up); [SOFREP](https://sofrep.com/specialoperations/noriega-surrendered-rock-panama-invasion-1989)
- **[MEDIUM] Waco, 1993 (FBI, not military, though military equipment was in support):** from about 22 March 1993 the FBI broadcast Nancy Sinatra songs, recordings of dying rabbits, Christmas carols and Tibetan chants all night to disrupt sleep. FBI behavioral experts warned in memos that the broadcasts would backfire. Negotiation coordinator Gary Noesner later said the tactic undermined negotiations. The "dentist drills" detail rests on secondary reports — [CESNUR (press archive)](https://www.cesnur.org/testi/waco51.htm); [Rod Dickinson re-enactment notes](https://roddickinson.net/pages/waco/project-video.php)
- **[HIGH] Guantánamo:** a 2004 DoD report said the "futility technique included the playing of Metallica, Britney Spears and Rap music". The 2005 Army Furlow-Schmidt investigation examined FBI allegations, including loud music. The 2008 Senate Armed Services Committee inquiry described detainees exposed to loud music (e.g., Drowning Pool's "Let the Bodies Hit the Floor"). FBI emails from 2004 describe interrogation rooms with "loud rock music" and strobe lights, calling such tactics "quite common" — [National Security Archive, "Music to Interrogate By" (22 Oct 2009)](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/news/20091022/index.htm); [ACLU memo on FBI documents](https://www.aclu.org/documents/aclu-interested-persons-memo-fbi-documents-concerning-detainee-abuse-guantanamo-bay?tab=misc); [The Torture Database (FBI GTMO survey email)](https://www.thetorturedatabase.org/document/email-response-gtmo-survey-8)
- **[MEDIUM] Fallujah 2004:** Marines mounted speakers on Humvees and played music (AC/DC, Eminem, Metallica) through the streets as a PSYOP against insurgents — [The New Press, Volcler, *Extremely Loud: Sound as a Weapon* (2013)](https://thenewpress.org/books/extremely-loud/); [book description](https://bookrastinating.com/book/611048/s/extremely-loud). Specific tracks ("Hells Bells") were not confirmed.

### Inferences
- The documented use of sound in PSYOP and interrogation works through psychology, sleep deprivation and annoyance, not through physiological "frequency" effects. Official records (Guantánamo, Panama) are strongest. Vietnam and Fallujah rest on veteran recollections and journalism.

### Gaps

## 6. WWII "Ghost Army" sonic deception (23rd Headquarters Special Troops)

### Takeaway

## 7. Naval sonar effects on humans and marine life: official findings

### Takeaway
The US Navy/NOAA Joint Interim Report (Dec 2001) concluded that Navy tactical mid-frequency sonar was the "most plausible source" of the acoustic trauma behind the March 2000 Bahamas beaked-whale strandings. For humans, Navy- and NSMRL-sponsored diver studies (1990s) set an interim limit for low-frequency (about 160–320 Hz) sonar exposure of 160 dB re 1 µPa, with aversion reactions appearing at lower levels.

### Cited Findings
- **[HIGH] Joint Interim Report, Bahamas Marine Mammal Stranding Event of 15–16 March 2000 (US Dept. of Commerce/NOAA and Secretary of the Navy, Dec 2001):** 17 cetaceans stranded, including 14 beaked whales. Necropsies found bleeding around the inner ears and, in one case, in the brain. Tactical mid-range frequency sonars in use during the exercise were "the most plausible source" of the trauma. Contributing factors were a strong surface duct, unusual bathymetry, intensive multi-sonar use, a constricted channel, and the presence of sensitive beaked whales — [NOAA repository report](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16198); [PDF](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16198/noaa_16198_DS1.pdf); [DOSITS strandings](https://dosits.org/animals/effects-of-sound/potential-effects-of-sound-on-marine-mammals/strandings). Animal People described it as the first time non-explosive underwater noise was shown to cause fatal trauma in marine mammals — [Animal People](https://newspaper.animalpeopleforum.org/?p=2126). Later reviews note it is unclear whether sound alone or other features of the exercise caused the strandings — [Cornell physiology PDF](https://physiology.med.cornell.edu/faculty/nirenberg/lab/pel/environment/sonar.pdf)
- **[HIGH] Navy diver studies (SURTASS LFA EIS program):** In 1993–95, tests by University of Texas ARL under NSMRL control exposed 87 subjects at 160 dB SPL with no long-term organ effects. Levels below 160 dB were not expected to cause physiological damage. In 1997–98 an ONR/NSMRL study found no damage at up to 157 dB. Only 2% of divers showed "very severe" aversion at 148 dB, and NSMRL lowered the limits by 3 dB as a safety margin — [Navy NEPA SURTASS LFA Diver Studies](https://www.nepa.navy.mil/surtass-lfa/diver-studies); [archived page](https://www.nepa.navy.mil/Completed-Projects/SURTASS-LFA-Sonar/diver-studies/)
- **[MEDIUM]** Interim Navy guidance for 160–320 Hz non-impulsive sound: at most 160 dB SPL for up to 100 s of continuous exposure at 50% duty cycle, with a cumulative 15 min per dive-day (Pestorius & Curley, ASA abstract) — [auditory.org ASA abstract](https://auditory.org/asamtgs/asa96ind/2pBV/2pBV1.html). An NSMRL report found open-water exposure no riskier than enclosed environments. DTIC reports: [ADA331727](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA331727.pdf); [ADA162404](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA162404.pdf). Review: [Evolution of Navy diver exposure standards](https://sonicfield.org/library/evolution-of-navy-diver-exposure-standards-for-deterministic-underwater-sound-in)
- **[LOW]** A patent filing states that diver aversion begins above 140 dB SPL in the low-frequency range — [USPTO patent PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12398279). A forum claim of a 145 dB LFA limit near divers is unverified.

### Inferences
- The Navy's own research established the human-diver thresholds. Marine-mammal harm from mid-frequency sonar is officially acknowledged for the Bahamas 2000 event. Sonar "dB" values are underwater (re 1 µPa) and **cannot be compared directly** with airborne dB (re 20 µPa) used for LRAD and similar devices: the reference pressures differ (about a 26 dB offset) and so do the acoustic impedances.

### Gaps
- Later litigation (*Winter v. NRDC*, 555 U.S. 7, 2008) and later strandings (Canary Islands 2002, Spain/Greece) were not retrieved here. The 2008 Supreme Court decision allowed Navy training to proceed; that is unverified in this session.

## 8. "Brown note" / infrasound bowel effects: debunking evidence

### Takeaway
No credible evidence supports a "brown note." MythBusters (Feb 2005) exposed a subject to about 120 dB at 9 Hz and up to 153 dB just above 20 Hz with no loss of bowel control, only chest vibration and shortness of breath. A 2007 *Military Medicine* literature review found no evidence that infrasound triggers diarrhea. The myth probably grew out of space-program whole-body *mechanical* vibration tests, not airborne sound.

### Cited Findings
- **[MEDIUM]** In the MythBusters episode (aired Feb 2005), levels reached about 120 dB at 9 Hz and up to 153 dB above 20 Hz, using an array of high-power subwoofers. Adam Savage did not lose bowel control; subjects reported anxiety and shortness of breath from air moving in the lungs. Verdict: "busted" — [Wikipedia: Brown note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_note); [MythResults episode 25](https://mythresults.com/episode25)
- **[MEDIUM]** A 2007 *Military Medicine* review of peer-reviewed literature found no evidence of infrasound triggering diarrhea. There is no reliable evidence for infrasound-induced nausea and vomiting. The myth's origin is linked to US space-program vibration studies using direct mechanical contact — [Wikipedia: Brown note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_note); [Sciencing](https://www.sciencing.com/2218338/brown-note-poop-myth-debunked-science/)
- **[LOW/MEDIUM]** Military commentary: the brown note was never a real riot-control capability — [Military.com 2021](https://www.military.com/off-duty/2021/01/06/why-brown-note-was-never-real-thing-riot-control.html)
- **[MEDIUM]** Consistent with Altmann's conclusion that infrasound lacks the alleged drastic effects — [Science & Global Security abstract](https://scienceandglobalsecurity.org/archive/2001/11/acoustic_weapons_-_a_prospecti.html)

### Inferences
- The brown note joins Gavreau's "lethal 7 Hz" as acoustic folklore. The evidence from military reviews, independent physicists and informal empirical tests is all negative.

- Primary NASA or US Air Force whole-body vibration data (resonance of abdominal organs at about 4–8 Hz under mechanical vibration) were not retrieved. Citing them would need the original AMRL/NASA technical reports.
