## Cross-cutting: What a granted patent does and does not prove

### Takeaway
A US patent shows that the USPTO found the claims novel, non-obvious, and adequately described, with a "credible" asserted utility. It does not show that the device was built, works as described, is safe, or was ever used. The utility bar is low: clinical or real-world efficacy is not required. The USPTO rejects only claims that are plainly impossible, such as perpetual motion, and it does not always catch non-working inventions.

### Cited Findings
- In *In re Brana* (Fed. Cir. 1995), the court held that FDA approval and successful human testing are not prerequisites for patent "utility." Early laboratory evidence was enough. — [Open Exam Prep utility guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/patent-bar/subject-matter-eligibility-utility/utility-requirements); [PTAB.us on Brana](https://www.ptab.us/2018/12/brana.html)
- The USPTO rejects inventions that contradict well-established laws of nature, such as perpetual motion and "cold fusion," for lack of credible utility (§101) and lack of enablement (§112(a)). Short of that, applicants need only "credible" evidence, not proof of effectiveness. — [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/patent-bar/subject-matter-eligibility-utility/utility-requirements); [Blue Iron IP](https://blueironip.com/ufaqs/how-does-the-uspto-handle-therapeutic-or-pharmacological-utility-claims/)
- Practitioners note that the Office "is not always able to identify non-functioning technology." — [Harness IP, "Can you patent something that doesn't work?"](https://www.harnessip.com/blog/2021/03/11/can-you-patent-something-that-doesnt-work/)
- Example of a patent misread in the wild: a 2023 Facebook post cited US 5,356,368 (a Monroe Institute meditation-audio patent) as proof that 5G towers are used for brainwashing. PolitiFact rated it "Pants on Fire": the patent is unrelated to 5G and expired in 2011. Check Your Fact found that the patent page "does not mention 5G anywhere." The Institute said its signals "are not broadcast over the air in any manner" and are delivered through ordinary audio. — [PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2023/oct/03/facebook-posts/a-us-patent-does-not-show-that-5g-towers-are-used/); [Check Your Fact](https://checkyourfact.com/2023/10/06/fact-check-patent-5g-towers-brainwashing)
- Lead Stories noted that the patent was held by a private company, Interstate Industries Inc., not the US government. — [Lead Stories](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2023/04/fact-check-video-does-not-show-gwen-tower-that-downloads-brainwaves.html) (as summarized in search index)
- Online compilations such as "40 Mind Control Patents" (archive.org PDF) and targeted-individual sites list many of the patents in this file (Puharich/Lawrence, Lowery, Loos, Brunkan, Air Force RF-hearing). They often describe them in stronger terms than the patent text. — [archive.org "40 Mind Control Patents"](https://ia904503.us.archive.org/34/items/40MindControlPatents_201703/40-Mind-Control-Patents.pdf); [Anti-Gang Stalking Center patent list](https://www.anti-gangstalking.center/index.php/concepts-theories/technology/usa-patents-for-mind-control-and-artificial-telepathy-technology.html)

### Inferences
- Most patents in this inventory belong to individual inventors or small companies and are expired. Expiry for non-payment of maintenance fees ("Expired – Fee Related") suggests the owner did not find the patent commercially worth keeping. That is weak evidence against large-scale use, not proof.
- "The government holds a patent" proves only that an agency employee filed an invention disclosure. It says nothing about deployment.

### Gaps
- I could not open the MPEP §2107 text directly. The legal summaries come from secondary law/study-guide sources.

## 2. "Silent sound" / subliminal: Lowery US 5,159,703

### Takeaway
US 5,159,703 claims a way to carry information (such as spoken affirmations) on a modulated carrier in the very low or very high audio range, or near-ultrasonic range, played through ordinary speakers or earphones. It is an acoustic-playback patent. The patent itself does not demonstrate that the subliminal content is perceived or that it changes behavior.

### Cited Findings
- **US 5,159,703: "Silent subliminal presentation system"** (Oliver M. Lowery; original assignee: Individual; filed Dec 28, 1989; granted Oct 27, 1992; status Expired – Fee Related). **[VERIFIED-index]** — [Google Patents US5159703A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5159703A/en); [Patent PDF (Google storage)](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/57/83/8a/a7ba63d45ea3f4/US5159703.pdf); [Justia inventor page](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/oliver-m-lowery)
- Abstract (paraphrased): a "silent communications system" in which carriers "in the very low or very high audio frequency range or in the adjacent ultrasonic frequency spectrum" are modulated with the intended information and propagated acoustically through loudspeakers, earphones, or piezoelectric transducers. The description gives an example carrier around 14,500 Hz and names modulation types including FM and upper single sideband with suppressed carrier. — [Google Patents US5159703A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5159703A/en); [Justia](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/oliver-m-lowery)
- The patent is widely cited online as a "Silent Sound mind control weapon." - Audio engineers have analyzed "silent subliminal" products as a form of signal hiding (steganography) and note practical playback limits. — [audionyq.com "Steganography and Silent Subliminals"](https://audionyq.com/steganography-and-silent-subliminals/); [Audacity forum thread](https://forum.audacityteam.org/t/silent-subliminal-presentation-system/61378)

### Inferences
- The patent requires a speaker or earphone near the listener. The claim text does not describe remote or covert broadcast over radio or cell networks.
- The assignee is "Individual." I found no record in patent data tying it to a government or company assignee (the oft-repeated "Silent Sounds Inc." link was not supported).

### Gaps

- I found no peer-reviewed validation of the claimed effect in this pass.

## 4. Hearing via non-acoustic routes (Puharich/Lawrence, Brunkan, US Air Force RF-hearing patents)

### Takeaway
Several patents claim ways to produce sound perception without airborne sound:
- Puharich and Lawrence: electrical or radio-frequency signals delivered via a tooth or the facial nerves, aimed at hearing aids for the deaf.
- Brunkan: pulsed, audio-modulated microwaves irradiating the head.
- Air Force (O'Loughlin and Loree): signal-processing and modulation schemes so that the microwave/RF auditory effect (the "Frey effect") conveys intelligible speech.

The underlying microwave-auditory effect is documented in the literature as a thermoelastic (thermoacoustic) phenomenon. The patents claim modulation methods. They do not prove fielded devices, long range, or covert use. The Air Force patents expired for fee non-payment.

## 5. Directional / parametric audio (Norris HyperSonic Sound, Pompei Audio Spotlight, LRAD)

LRAD acoustic hailing devices are high-power directional loudspeakers, a separate patent family.

## 7. Loos patents: "subliminal acoustic manipulation of nervous systems" and relatives

### Takeaway
Hendricus G. Loos holds a family of late-1990s patents claiming that weak periodic stimuli (subaudio acoustic pulses, weak magnetic or electric fields, thermal stimuli) near "sensory resonance" frequencies of about ½ Hz and 2.5 Hz can affect the autonomic nervous system, causing effects such as sleepiness. These are individual-inventor patents that rest on the inventor's own hypothesis of "sensory resonances." The patent grant does not validate the effect. The patents are among the most quoted in "mind control" lists.

## 8. Recent (2015–2026) audio neuromodulation patents: 40 Hz gamma (MIT / Cognito Therapeutics)

### Takeaway
MIT (Li-Huei Tsai and colleagues) holds granted US patents on delivering ~40 Hz auditory (and combined audio-visual) stimulation to induce gamma oscillations, aimed at Alzheimer's-type pathology. These are licensed to Cognito Therapeutics, whose Spectris device holds FDA Breakthrough Device Designation, which is not approval. The pivotal HOPE trial (n≈670) was completed by 2026. As of this research I found no topline results or FDA marketing authorization.

### Cited Findings
- **US 10,265,497**, from application 15/360,637 filed Nov 23, 2016, issued Apr 23, 2019 (MIT gamma-stimulation family parent). **[PARTIAL]** Number and dates come from continuation text in the index. The title was not seen. — [Google Patents US10682490B2 (family text)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10682490B2/en)
- **US 10,682,490 B2: "Methods and devices for providing a stimulus to a subject to induce gamma oscillations"** (inventors: Li-Huei Tsai, Emery Brown, Hannah Iaccarino, Anthony James Martorell, Chinnakkaruppan Adaikkan; MIT). It covers auditory click trains at roughly 35–45 clicks/s to reduce amyloid-β in the hippocampus or auditory cortex. **[VERIFIED-index]** — [Google Patents US10682490B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10682490B2/en)
- **US 12,296,106 B2**, same title (continuation). **[VERIFIED-index]** — [Google Patents US12296106B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12296106B2/en)
- **US 12,318,549 B2** (MIT; filed Jun 15, 2020; granted Jun 3, 2025), a continuation in the family. **[VERIFIED-index]** (USPTO Official Gazette) — [USPTO Patents Gazette](https://patentsgazette.uspto.gov/week22/OG/html/1535-1/US12318549-20250603.html)
- **US 12,311,194** (MIT; granted May 27, 2025) claims combined auditory and visual stimuli at about 20–60 Hz, specifically about 40 Hz. **[PARTIAL]** — [Justia, Li-Huei Tsai](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/li-huei-tsai)
- Cognito, co-founded by Tsai and Ed Boyden, licenses MIT technology. *The Transmitter* reports it has been awarded 11 US patents and raised $128M. — [The Transmitter](https://www.thetransmitter.org/brain-stimulation/putting-a-bright-idea-to-the-test/)
- MIT showed in 2019 (*Cell*) that 40 Hz sound stimulation works in mice. The method is called GENUS. — [MIT News 2023](https://news.mit.edu/2023/40-hz-vibrations-reduce-alzheimers-pathology-symptoms-mouse-models-0605); [MIT Picower GENUS](https://picower.mit.edu/innovations-inventions/genus)
- Spectris has FDA Breakthrough Device Designation. HOPE enrolled 670 participants. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05637801 lists the trial as completed (last update Sept 2026) with no results posted. The small OVERTURE study reported large percentage reductions in decline, which commentators flag as limited by small samples and post-hoc analyses. — [FierceBiotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/cognito-therapeutics-nets-fda-breakthrough-label-for-light-sound-therapy-for-alzheimer-s); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05637801](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05637801); [Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/cognito-therapeutics-completes-enrollment-in-landmark-alzheimer-s-trial); [BioSpace AD/PD 2026](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/cognito-therapeutics-presents-new-data-on-spectris-in-alzheimers-disease-at-ad-pd-2026)
- MIT News (Nov 2025) reports that some patients may benefit for years in a small follow-up study. — [MIT News 2025](https://news.mit.edu/2025/study-suggests-40hz-sensory-stimulation-may-benefit-some-alzheimers-patients-1114)

### Inferences
- This is the clearest example of an "audio frequency affects the brain" patent backed by a regulated clinical program. Even here, the patents themselves prove nothing about efficacy, and the definitive human evidence (HOPE) was still pending public results as of late October 2026.
