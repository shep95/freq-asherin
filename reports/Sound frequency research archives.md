# Archives Show Sound's Real Power Is Mundane

The declassified, military and patent record on sound does not show any covert "frequency weapon" that controls minds or injures people silently. What it documents is narrower. **Loud audible sound** hurts hearing and works well as a stressor. **Repetition, sleep deprivation and fear** work through psychology. **One real but tightly bounded physical effect**, microwave hearing, lets pulsed radio energy be perceived as clicks inside the head. Intelligence agencies did take an interest in binaural audio (the Army's 1983 Gateway analysis and the Monroe Hemi-Sync contract), in "sub-aural" sound (MKULTRA Subproject 54, which was never carried out) and in Soviet "psychotronics." None of these files records a measured, working capability. The Havana Syndrome "sonic attack" recordings were matched to a cricket. Official bodies then moved to pulsed radiofrequency (RF) energy, not sound, as the leading hypothesis. In June 2026 the Director of National Intelligence (DNI) rescinded the skeptical intelligence assessments, so the question is officially open again with no new causal finding. The patents people cite as proof (Lowery's "silent subliminal," Loos's "subliminal acoustic manipulation," the Air Force RF-hearing patents) show only that the USPTO accepted the claims as novel. Patent law does not require proof that an invention works. For shepherd.freq this sets a clear boundary. Binaural beats have a small, real but inconsistent evidence base for short-term anxiety and acute pain. 40 Hz stimulation is a serious but unproven medical research program that a phone tone does not replicate. Infrasound, ultrasound, Schumann, 432/528 Hz and "PEMF" sound claims are either unsupported or physically impossible on consumer speakers. Hearing safety is the one area where the evidence is strong and directly applicable.

## Four kinds of evidence carry very different weight

The topic mixes four kinds of source that are often treated as equal, and they are not. A **declassified document** proves that someone in government wrote it: a proposal, a theory, a contract, a press clipping. It proves a capability only if it contains test results. An **official assessment** (National Academies, JASON, the ODNI, the Senate Intelligence Committee) is a weighed judgment and can be revised or rescinded. A **granted patent** proves less than most people assume. Under *In re Brana* (Fed. Cir. 1995), human testing is not a prerequisite for patent "utility." The USPTO rejects only inventions that contradict established physical law, such as perpetual motion ([Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/patent-bar/subject-matter-eligibility-utility/utility-requirements); [PTAB.us on Brana](https://www.ptab.us/2018/12/brana.html)). Patent practitioners acknowledge that the Office "is not always able to identify non-functioning technology" ([Harness IP](https://www.harnessip.com/blog/2021/03/11/can-you-patent-something-that-doesnt-work/)). **Peer-reviewed science** is the only category built to answer "does it work?", and its quality varies widely.

The table below classifies the major claims in this space by the strongest evidence that supports them.

## Intelligence files document curiosity, not capability

The CIA's MKULTRA records contain surprisingly little acoustic material, and part of the reason is that **most MKULTRA files were destroyed in 1973 on DCI Richard Helms's order** ([Wikipedia, MKUltra](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKULTRA)). Two acoustic items survive in the record. **Subproject 54 ("Perfect Concussion")**, a Navy-linked proposal, asked whether "sub-aural frequency blasts" could erase memory. DCI Stansfield Turner told Congress in 1977 that it was cancelled and "never carried out" ([Wikipedia, MKUltra](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/MKULTRA)). **Subproject 68** funded research on "the effects upon human behavior of the repetition of verbal signals." This is the documented basis of Ewen Cameron's "psychic driving," in which patients heard taped messages repeated, combined with drugs and sensory deprivation ([CIA Reading Room](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CONTINUATION%20OF%20MKULTRA%2C%20%5B10427391%5D.pdf)). The documented auditory component of MKULTRA is therefore about **content and repetition, not frequency**. Claims of a working subliminal-audio or infrasound program circulate on forums with no supporting CIA document.

The most viral document in this space is the **"Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process" (9 June 1983, 29 pages)**. Lt. Col. Wayne M. McDonnell of Army INSCOM wrote it, and it is held in the CIA's STARGATE collection as CIA-RDP96-00788R001700210016-5 ([The Black Vault](https://www.theblackvault.com/documentarchive/stargate-collection-analysis-and-assessment-of-gateway-process-9-june-1983/)). It explains the Monroe Institute's Hemi-Sync method, binaural-beat audio intended to bring the brain hemispheres into coherence, through a speculative mix of physics, holography and consciousness theory. It is a theoretical write-up, not an experiment. The documentation around it is real. CIA Reading Room memos record that INSCOM's Center Lane office gave Robert Monroe a **personal services contract in July 1983** to test Hemi-Sync for "psychoenergetic" collection, with four training phases. The same records show plans to use it for screening, managerial training and language learning ([CIA Reading Room 271637](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/print/271637); [CIA Reading Room 1521993](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/print/1521993)). The CIA said in a 2021 FOIA response that it could not locate page 25 ([The Black Vault](https://www.theblackvault.com/documentarchive/stargate-collection-analysis-and-assessment-of-gateway-process-9-june-1983/)). No document found shows that Hemi-Sync improved remote-viewer performance. The remote-viewing program as a whole was **terminated in 1995** after an American Institutes for Research review found no demonstrated operational value ([Wikipedia, Stargate Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_Project_(U.S._Army_unit))). An honest summary: Army intelligence was interested in binaural audio, paid for training, and theorized about it. The archives contain no result.

The **"Moscow Signal"** is often folded into the "sonic attack" story, but it was **microwave radiation, not sound**. From the 1950s to the 1970s the Soviets beamed it at the US Embassy at about 5 microwatts per cm². The Pentagon's Project PANDORA, which included BIZARRE primate studies and cost **$4,615,000** according to a 1977 DARPA letter, never established injury, and the signal was ultimately attributed to eavesdropping ([NSA Archive document 06](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/ri5s7t-pi5hv/06.pdf); [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2017/08/25/the-secret-history-of-diplomats-and-invisible-weapons-russia-cuba/)). Western archives also hold second-hand US characterizations of Soviet-bloc work. A Reuters story on a DIA report, clipped into CIA files, warned that Soviet and Czech "psychotronic" research had weapon potential, and it also noted that skeptics called such reports disinformation ([CIA Reading Room 306575](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/print/306575)). This research found **no primary Stasi documents** on acoustic weapons, so claims of Stasi "Zersetzung" by noise remain unverified.

The **best-documented use of sound on people by a US intelligence agency is ordinary loud noise**. The Senate Intelligence Committee's 2014 Executive Summary describes detainees at the CIA site COBALT held in darkness, shackled, "with loud noise or music and only a bucket to use for human waste." It also documents sleep deprivation of up to 180 hours, with at least five detainees hallucinating. Its Finding #1 states that the techniques "were not an effective means of acquiring intelligence or gaining cooperation" ([SSCI Findings and Conclusions](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/sites-default-files-press-findings-and-conclusions.pdf)). CIA Director Brennan disputed the effectiveness conclusion ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/cia-mistreated-detainees-misrepresented-intelligence-mismanaged-program-senate-probe-finds.amp)).

## Havana's "sonic attack" became crickets, then radio waves, then an open question

The Havana Syndrome record shows why the acoustic framing collapsed. The State Department's 2018 Accountability Review Board, declassified in 2021, found the injury pattern "suggests a targeted action" but named no cause. The "sonic" label came from the press, not the board ([Unredacted/NSArchive](https://unredacted.com/2021/11/19/2021-documents-in-review-declassified-state-department-review-of-the-havana-syndrome/)). The **JASON** scientific advisory group analyzed the audio recordings in 2018. It found that sounds in at least 8 of 21 incidents were "most likely" the **Indies short-tailed cricket**, and it concluded with high confidence that the audio was **not** produced by RF or ultrasound ([FAS/IRP copy of JASON report](https://irp.fas.org/agency/dod/jason/havana.pdf); [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/danvergano/havana-syndrome-jason-crickets)). Stubbs and Montealegre-Zapata independently matched the AP recording to *Anurogryllus celerinictus* in pulse rate, spectrum and echo structure ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/510834v1)). Weapons acoustician Jürgen Altmann said he knew of no acoustic effect that would produce concussion-like symptoms ([Fox News](https://foxnews.com/tech/sonic-weapons-reported-in-cuba-could-sound-be-harnessed-in-an-attack.amp)).

Expert bodies then turned to electromagnetic energy. The **National Academies (December 2020)** judged "directed, pulsed radio frequency energy" the "most plausible mechanism" for the distinctive acute symptoms, while naming no source and not concluding that an attack occurred ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2020/12/05/world/report-points-microwave-attack-likely-source-mystery-illnesses-that-hit-diplomats-spies); [KPBS/NPR](https://www.kpbs.org/news/2020/12/06/microwave-radiation-most-plausible-cause-of)). The **IC Experts Panel (February 2022)** found that pulsed RF "plausibly explains the core characteristics" of a small subset of cases. It kept **ultrasound** as possible only in limited close-range cases, which is the last surviving acoustic thread ([ODNI executive summary](https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/2022%5F02%5F01%5FAHI%5FExecutive%5FSummary%5FFINAL%5FRedacted.pdf); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/national-security/havana-syndrome-symptoms-small-group-likely-caused-directed-energy-say-rcna14584)). The **March 2023 Intelligence Community Assessment** went the other way: most agencies judged a foreign adversary "very unlikely" to be responsible ([DNI statement](https://www.dni.gov/index.php/newsroom/press-releases/press-releases-2023/3674-intelligence-community-assessment-on-anomalous-health-incidents)). **NIH studies in JAMA (March 2024)** found no MRI-detectable brain injury in 81 affected participants compared with 48 controls, while acknowledging that the symptoms were real ([NIH](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/nih-studies-find-severe-symptoms-havana-syndrome-no-evidence-mri-detectable-brain-injury-or-biological-abnormalities)). The **updated assessment (released January 2025)** kept five of seven agencies at "very unlikely," with two shifting to a low-confidence "roughly even chance" involving a novel weapon ([ODNI updated assessment](https://archive.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/NIC-Unclassified-ICA-Updated-Assessment-AHI-December2024.pdf)). A House Intelligence Committee interim report called the 2023 assessment "highly irregular" ([House Intelligence Committee](https://intelligence.house.gov/UploadedFiles/Unclassified_AHI_Report.pdf)).

On **June 12, 2026, DNI Tulsi Gabbard rescinded both the 2023 and 2025 assessments**. A memo said they had selectively excluded intelligence, suppressed alternative analysis and relied on an "ethically flawed medical study" ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/12/politics/gabbard-rescinds-biden-era-intel-assessments-that-were-skeptical-about-havana-syndrome); [AP via Baltimore Sun](https://www.baltimoresun.com/2026/06/12/gabbard-rescinds-intelligence-assessments-on-mysterious-havana-syndrome/)). The rescission was procedural. It added no new physical evidence, and no replacement assessment had been found as of late October 2026. The current state is therefore: the recorded sounds were insects; audible sound has been dropped as the injuring agent; pulsed RF is the leading but unproven hypothesis; and no standing intelligence assessment exists.

## Microwave hearing is real physics with a narrow window

The **microwave auditory effect** is the one exotic phenomenon in this space that rests on solid, replicated science. Allan Frey reported it in the *Journal of Applied Physiology* in 1962 ([PubMed 13895081](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13895081/)). Foster and Finch (*Science*, 1974) established the accepted thermoelastic mechanism ([PubMed 4833827](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4833827/)). A short pulse of RF energy heats head tissue by a tiny amount, on the order of **a millionth of a degree**. The resulting expansion launches a pressure wave that the cochlea hears as a click, buzz or chirp ([ARPANSA review](https://www.arpansa.gov.au/sites/default/files/legacy/pubs/rps/aud_perc.pdf)). Perception depends on **energy per pulse, not average power**. Perceived pitch is set by the head's own acoustic resonance, not by the radio frequency ([Elder & Chou 2003](https://waves.lima-city.de/cms/../dok/Auditory%20Response%20to%20Pulsed%20Radiofrequency%20Energy%20-%20Elder%20and%20Chou.pdf)). Because the sound is generated inside the tissue, **there is no airborne sound for a microphone or bystander to detect**. This is why the recorded Havana audio cannot have been the Frey effect ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/politics/politics-news/havana-syndrome-crickets-not-microwaves-report-1235163/)), and why speakers, phone apps and ultrasonic emitters cannot produce it.

The military record here is documented but modest. The claim that Walter Reed researchers Sharp and Grove transmitted spoken words (c. 1973) comes from Don Justesen's 1975 *American Psychologist* article, not from any primary publication. As relayed, listeners recognized spoken digits one to ten, and longer speech was not attempted because exposures would approach safety limits ([ja.wikid.org, citing Justesen](https://ja.wikid.org/%E3%83%9E%E3%82%A4%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AD%E6%B3%A2%E8%81%B4%E8%A6%9A%E5%8A%B9%E6%9E%9C)). A 1998 Army document, "Bioeffects of Selected Non-Lethal Weapons," released by FOIA and publicized in 2008, discussed RF hearing as a phenomenon of non-lethal interest and reported no permanent effects ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2008-02-pentagon-lasers-voices.html)). The Navy-funded **MEDUSA** concept ("Mob Excess Deterrent Using Silent Audio") did not advance beyond early-phase funding. Biophysicists told IEEE Spectrum it "cannot work" because dangerous heating would come before the sound became bothersome ([ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/Technology/AheadoftheCurve/story?id=5305386); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/why-microwave-auditory-effect-crowdcontrol-gun-wont-work)).

The live scientific dispute concerns intensity, not existence. James Lin, who developed much of the theory, argued in 2021 that pulsed microwaves could plausibly have been delivered covertly in Havana at discomfort-level sound pressures. Foster, Garrett and Ziskin answered that loud perception requires energy per pulse at levels where **tissue heating becomes the dominant hazard**, and that no exposure was ever detected, making weaponization implausible ([Frontiers in Public Health 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.788613/pdf)). Current safety standards (IEEE C95.1-2019, ICNIRP 2020) explicitly take microwave hearing into account for pulsed exposures ([IEEE OEB slides](https://ewh.ieee.org/r6/oeb/lm/previous_meetings/2019/Chou%20May%2029%202019.pdf)). Lin criticizes those limits, and standards-committee members dispute his critique ([PMC12961997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12961997/)). No peer-reviewed controlled study shows sustained, intelligible, loud speech delivered this way, and none supports individual "voice-to-skull" harassment claims.

## Militaries weaponized loudness and fear, not secret frequencies

Independent assessments of acoustic weapons reach the same conclusion. Physicist Jürgen Altmann's 2001 review in *Science & Global Security* found that infrasound "does not have the alleged drastic effects on humans." It also found that pain or balance effects "seem unachievable at ranges above about 50 m for meter-size sources," and that the main real risk is **hearing damage** ([Science & Global Security](https://scienceandglobalsecurity.org/archive/2001/11/acoustic_weapons_-_a_prospecti.html)). His earlier review put marked non-auditory effects only above **150 dB** in the low audio range ([ASA Pressroom](https://acoustics.org/pressroom/httpdocs/137th/altmann.html)). The US National Research Council (2003) called acoustic-weapon effectiveness unproven ([NAP](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/10538/chapter/9)). NATO's human-effects group (2006) found "very little relevant quality data," much of it restricted ([NATO TR-HFM-073](https://www.sto.nato.int/publications/STO%20Technical%20Reports/RTO-TR-HFM-073/$$TR-HFM-073-ALL.pdf)). Two infrasound legends fall apart under this scrutiny. Acoustician Geoff Leventhall writes that 1960s researcher Vladimir Gavreau "confused harmful effects of very high levels at higher frequencies with the effects of infrasound." The press then added the "fatal 7 Hz" claim ([IOA proceedings](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/g_leventhall_thirty_five_years_of_low_frequency_noise.pdf)), and one of his whistles was about 2,600 Hz, which is not infrasonic at all ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/you-cant-blow-somebodys-brain-up-with-sound-141239932)). The "brown note" failed a 2005 MythBusters test at up to 153 dB, and a 2007 *Military Medicine* review found no evidence for it ([Wikipedia: Brown note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_note)).

The fielded acoustic devices are **directional loudspeakers working in the main band of human hearing**. Acoustic hailing devices grew out of a Navy requirement after the **2000 USS Cole bombing**, where the crew could not be sure an approaching boat heard their warnings ([Wikipedia: LRAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-range_acoustic_device)). The manufacturer says they are optimized for **1–5 kHz** ([Genasys](https://genasys.com/blog/lrad-is-the-de-facto-standard-for-long-range-hailing-devices-and-heres-why)). In a 2009 Marine demonstration one relayed intelligible messages at 300 m ([GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2009/04/mil-090402-mcn01.htm)). The injuries are documented in court. Pittsburgh paid **$72,000** to a professor who reported permanent hearing damage from LRAD use at the 2009 G-20 ([ACLU-PA](https://www.aclupa.org/press-releases/city-pittsburgh-settles-g-20-lawsuits/)). In ***Edrei v. Maguire* (2d Cir., June 13, 2018)**, the court held that using an LRAD "in a manner capable of causing serious injury to move non-violent protesters" can be unconstitutional excessive force. Plaintiffs reported auditory pain, migraines, tinnitus and hearing loss ([Opinion via SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/18-810-opinion-below.pdf)). Physicians for Human Rights recommends suspending acoustic weapons at protests ([Lethal in Disguise](https://lethalindisguise.org/crowd-control-weapons/acoustic-weapons/)). Israel's "Scream," first used at Bil'in in June 2005, left protesters with dizziness and nausea, and no specifications were ever released ([GlobalSecurity/AP](https://www.globalsecurity.org/org/news/2005/050610-sound-weapon.htm)). The UK **"Mosquito"** emits about **17.4 kHz**, a tone mostly audible to young people. The Council of Europe Parliamentary Assembly recommended banning it in public places in 2010 on dignity and discrimination grounds, while noting that research so far showed **no hearing-loss danger** ([PACE news](https://pace.coe.int/news/3000/pace-calls-for-ban-on-acoustic-youth-dispersal-devices)).

Psychological operations relied on **meaning, not frequency**. Vietnam's "Ghost Tape No. 10" exploited beliefs about wandering souls ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2023/09/16/vietnam-war-psyops-ghosts/)). Troops blasted rock music at Noriega's refuge in 1989 ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/judgment-day-heavy-metal-s-role-manuel-noriega-s-surrender-n766411)). The FBI's noise broadcasts at Waco went ahead despite its own behavioral experts' warnings ([CESNUR](https://www.cesnur.org/testi/waco51.htm)). Official records confirm music as an interrogation "futility technique" at Guantánamo ([National Security Archive](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/news/20091022/index.htm)). The WWII Ghost Army played recordings of troops and vehicles from halftrack speakers to fake whole divisions ([Ghost Army Legacy Project](https://ghostarmy.org/about/history)). The Navy's own research does document physiological thresholds underwater. A Navy/NOAA report named mid-frequency sonar the "most plausible source" of the 2000 Bahamas beaked-whale strandings ([NOAA](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/16198)). Navy diver studies set a 160 dB (re 1 µPa) interim limit for low-frequency sonar, with aversion appearing at lower levels ([Navy NEPA](https://www.nepa.navy.mil/surtass-lfa/diver-studies)). These underwater decibel figures use a different reference and cannot be compared with airborne decibels.

## Patents prove novelty, not that anything works

The patents most often cited as proof of "mind control" mostly belong to individuals, are expired, and describe ordinary acoustic playback or a lab phenomenon. **Robert Monroe's US 5,356,368**, "Method of and apparatus for inducing desired states of consciousness," covers headphone or speaker audio and expired in 2011. PolitiFact rated "Pants on Fire" a 2023 claim that it proves 5G towers brainwash people, and the Monroe Institute said its signals "are not broadcast over the air in any manner" ([PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2023/oct/03/facebook-posts/a-us-patent-does-not-show-that-5g-towers-are-used/)). His earlier US 3,884,218 (1975) claimed sleep induction using audio modulated at sleep-EEG rhythms ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3884218)). **Oliver Lowery's US 5,159,703**, "Silent subliminal presentation system" (1992, individual assignee, expired for unpaid fees), claims messages carried on modulated tones at the extremes of the audio band or just above it, **played through ordinary loudspeakers or earphones**. It does not describe remote broadcast and contains no evidence that the hidden content is perceived ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5159703A/en)). **Hendricus Loos's US 6,017,302**, "Subliminal acoustic manipulation of nervous systems" (2000), and its relatives rest on the inventor's own hypothesis of "sensory resonances" near ½ Hz and 2.5 Hz. No independent replication was found ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6017302A/en)).

The RF-hearing patents are real but limited in the same way. Puharich and Lawrence's 1960s–70s patents were **hearing aids for the deaf**, using tooth or facial-nerve stimulation ([Google Patents US3170993](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3170993A/en)). Brunkan's US 4,877,027 (individual inventor) claims hearing sensations from audio-modulated microwave pulses, supported only by the inventor's assertion ([Justia](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4877027)). The **Air Force's US 6,470,214 (2002) and US 6,587,729** describe signal processing intended to make the Frey effect carry intelligible speech ([Justia](https://patents.justia.com/patent/6470214); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6587729B2/en)). Both have the status **"Expired – Fee Related,"** meaning the government stopped paying to keep them. That is weak evidence against a fielded program, though it says nothing about classified work. Directional-audio patents (Norris's HyperSonic Sound, Pompei's Audio Spotlight) cover the "sound from ultrasound" principle, in which air itself turns a modulated ultrasonic beam back into a narrow audible beam ([Wikipedia, Sound from ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_from_ultrasound)). The result is **ordinary audible sound that anyone standing in the beam hears and any microphone records**, not a private neural channel. At the credible end, MIT holds granted patents on **40 Hz auditory and audiovisual gamma stimulation** (e.g., US 10,682,490) licensed to Cognito Therapeutics ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10682490B2/en)). This is the only patent family here backed by a regulated clinical program, and the patents still prove nothing about efficacy.

## Peer-reviewed science supports a few modest claims

For the frequencies a consumer app actually plays, the evidence is uneven. **Binaural beats** have the strongest base. A 2019 meta-analysis of 22 studies found a medium pooled effect (**g = 0.45**) on memory, attention, anxiety and pain, with longer pre-task exposure helping ([PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/)). The claimed mechanism, however, fails as often as it holds. In a 2023 systematic review of EEG entrainment, **5 studies supported it and 8 contradicted it** ([PMC10198548](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10198548/)). A 2024 review of pain trials rated the evidence low to very low quality, with a possible acute-pain benefit ([BMC CMT 2024](https://bmccomplementmedtherapies.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12906-024-04339-y)). **Isochronic tones** do drive a measurable auditory steady-state response ([ABE 2013](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/abe/2/0/2_55/_article)), but only 2 of 17 quality trials in one review tested them ([Rev Mex Neurocienc](https://www.scielo.org.mx/pdf/rmn/v22n6/2604-6180-rmn-22-6-238.pdf)). So the Army's 1983 interest in Hemi-Sync and today's meta-analyses tell a consistent story: there is a plausible, modest effect, and "entraining the brain" is not a reliable mechanism.

**40 Hz gamma stimulation** is contested even in mice. A Buzsáki-lab replication (Soula et al., 2023) found no amyloid reduction and no robust gamma entrainment ([Alzforum](https://alzforum.org/node/2701666)). The pivotal **HOPE trial (n = 673)** of Cognito's combined light-and-sound device completed in August 2026, and no results were published as of late October 2026 ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05637801](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05637801)). Even a positive result would validate a supervised medical device, not a phone tone. On **infrasound**, a 2023 double-blind trial exposed 37 adults to simulated wind-turbine infrasound for 72 hours and found **no adverse effects**, while traffic noise did disrupt sleep ([PMC10174642](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10174642)). Vic Tandy's 18.98 Hz "ghost frequency" consists of two uncontrolled case reports in a parapsychology journal ([Skeptic's Dictionary](https://skepdic.com/infrasound.html)). **432 Hz and 528 Hz** claims rest on pilots of 9 to 12 people, some co-authored by an alternative-tuning advocate ([Acta Biomedica](https://mattioli1885journals.com/index.php/actabiomedica/article/view/10755); [SCIRP](https://www.scirp.org/html/2-8204397_87146.htm)). No primary literature supports "528 Hz repairs DNA." The **Schumann resonance** (about 7.83 Hz) is a real electromagnetic phenomenon, and the biological claims are correlational or theoretical ([PubMed 40394813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40394813/)). A 7.83 Hz audio tone is a pressure wave, not an EM field. In the same way, "PEMF frequencies" played as sound lack the magnetic-induction mechanism of FDA-approved PEMF bone stimulators ([PMC5822965](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5822965)). Medical **focused ultrasound** is FDA-approved for tremor, but it works by thermal ablation using MHz transducers coupled to the scalp ([FDA P150038](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf15/P150038S006D.pdf)). A phone capped near 22 kHz cannot replicate it.

High-frequency sound and hearing safety are where the science bears most directly on an app. Tim Leighton documented widespread public exposure to very-high-frequency and ultrasonic sound, with complaints of nausea, migraine and tinnitus. He says causation is unproven and that exposure limits were set without data from young people ([Royal Society A](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/the-sounds-of-silence-might-be-making-us-suffer/Ultrasound-in-the-air_Royal-Soc-A_Paper.pdf); [JASA editorial](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/423687/1/2018_Leighton_JASA_Editorial_Ultrasound_in_air_version_post_review.pdf)). Hearing-dose limits are firm: **NIOSH 85 dB(A) over 8 hours** with a 3 dB exchange rate ([CDC Stacks](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/128772/cdc_128772_DS1.pdf)), and the **WHO/ITU safe-listening standard of 80 dB(A) for 40 hours a week for adults and 75 dB(A) for children** ([PMC10194210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10194210/)). Sound can shatter glass, as MythBusters showed at about 556 Hz and 105 dB with lead crystal after many attempts ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/fact-or-fiction-opera-singer-can-shatter-glass/)). That level is far beyond a phone speaker.

## Conclusion

The archive inverts the popular story. The government files people treat as proof of secret frequency technology mostly record **proposals, theories and contracts that produced no documented result**. The verified harms from sound are low-tech: loudness that injures ears, noise that denies sleep, and recordings that frighten. The one exotic effect that is real, microwave hearing, is electromagnetic, inaudible to anyone but the exposed person, and limited by heating. No app can produce it. The record has two genuinely open items. The cause of Havana Syndrome has been officially unresolved since the June 2026 rescission, and the HOPE trial's 40 Hz results are still unpublished. Both should be re-checked before anything the app publishes depends on them.

For shepherd.freq, the defensible position follows from the evidence tiers. Present the archives accurately: Gateway and Hemi-Sync as documented Army *interest*, not a capability, and the patents as *claims*. Offer binaural beats for relaxation and focus without promising "brainwave entrainment." Present 432/528 Hz, Solfeggio and Schumann as tradition or preference, not medicine. Do not imply that phone audio can deliver infrasound, ultrasound, PEMF or 40 Hz therapy. Hearing safety is the strongest evidence in this entire field, so the most valuable feature the app can build from it is protective: volume caps, fade-ins, dose tracking against the WHO 80 dB(A) weekly standard, and warnings that high-frequency tones an adult cannot hear may still reach children and pets.
